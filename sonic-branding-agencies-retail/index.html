What retail brands need from sound

A retailer is heard in more places than most brands: in ads, in the app, at the till and in the shop itself.

One sound across ads, app and stores

  • The same motif should end the TV spot, open the social video and play in the app. Without a system, every campaign starts its music from zero. That was the starting point at Douglas.

Short formats first

  • Most retail advertising is short. A sonic logo of about three seconds and a one-note cue for the first second of a video carry the brand where a full track cannot.

Music in the shop

  • In-store music sets pace and mood. In Germany, playing music in a shop usually needs a GEMA licence, so plan the rights together with the playlist.

Proof in numbers

  • Ask for results from retail work. OTTO’s sonic identity lifted brand image among Gen Z and young millennials by 28% and view-through rate by 21%.

What does sonic branding for a retailer cost?

EightSix publishes these ranges: about €15,000 to €30,000 for a sonic logo project and €50,000 to over €500,000 for a complete sonic identity system. The price depends on scope, complexity and usage rights.

Do retailers need a licence to play music in stores?

In Germany, usually yes: playing music in a shop is a public performance and normally needs a GEMA licence. Ask the agency how in-store use of your brand music is covered before you sign.

How long does a sonic identity for a retail brand take?

A sonic identity project at EightSix usually takes 8 to 16 weeks: two to three weeks of research, four to eight weeks of composition, two to three weeks of testing and one to two weeks for guidelines.
