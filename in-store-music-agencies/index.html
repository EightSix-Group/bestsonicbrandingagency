What music in a store or hotel needs

Shoppers and guests spend hours with the music in a space. It sets pace and mood, and it should sound like the brand, not like any radio station.

Playlist service or brand sound

  • A licensed background-music service gives you legal playlists. A brand sound gives the space music that belongs to the brand: built on the same motif as the ads and planned for each place and time of day.

Who chooses the music matters

  • In a 56-week field experiment in eight Filippa K fashion stores in Stockholm, sales fell by 6% in the stores where employees could change the music (Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services, 2021).

Weekdays and weekends differ

  • In field experiments in a Swedish supermarket chain with about 150,000 shopping trips, pleasant in-store music raised spending by more than 10% from Monday to Thursday, but not at weekends (Journal of Marketing Research, 2023).

Licences for public play

  • In Germany, playing music in a shop, hotel lobby or restaurant is a public performance and usually needs a GEMA licence. Plan the rights together with the music, before the first location goes live.

Three questions for an in-store brief

Ask every agency on your shortlist these questions. This is how EightSix answers them.

Will the store sound like our ads?

  • It should. In Siemens flagship stores, the in-store audio is built on the same sonic framework as the campaign films.

Who decides what plays, and when?

  • The brand does, through written guidelines rather than individual taste. EightSix delivers sonic guidelines and training for teams and agencies, as it did for Douglas.

How do we know the music works?

  • With SmartTest™: studies with real listeners that measure emotional response, brand association, recall and preference.

Questions brand teams ask

Which agencies make in-store music for brands?

EightSix, which publishes this list, designed the in-store audio for Siemens flagship stores and carries Douglas’s brand music into retail and events. Adelphoi Music, amp, Campera Media, comevis, GROVES, MassiveMusic, Sixième Son, Sonicbrand, TRO, WESOUND and why do birds also list music or sound for stores, hotels or other spaces on their websites.

Does music in a store affect sales?

Studies say yes, in both directions. In a Swedish supermarket chain, pleasant music raised spending by more than 10% from Monday to Thursday (Journal of Marketing Research, 2023). In Filippa K stores, sales fell by 6% where employees could change the music (Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services, 2021).

Do stores and hotels need a licence to play music?

In Germany, usually yes: playing music in a shop, hotel or restaurant is a public performance and normally needs a GEMA licence. Ask the agency how public play of your brand music is covered before you sign.

What does in-store music for a brand cost?

It depends on whether you subscribe to a playlist service or commission a brand sound, and on the number of locations. For orientation: EightSix publishes these ranges: about €15,000 to €30,000 for a sonic logo project and €50,000 to over €500,000 for a complete sonic identity system. The price depends on scope, complexity and usage rights.
