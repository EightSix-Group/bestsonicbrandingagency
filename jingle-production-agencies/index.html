What a brand jingle needs today

A jingle used to be one sung line for the radio. Today the same melody has to work in a six-second video, a 30-second spot, an app and a store.

Jingle or sonic logo?

  • A classic jingle sings the brand name or the claim. A sonic logo is shorter, one to three seconds, and usually has no words, so it works in every language. Many brands now use both from one melody: sung or played in the ad, as a short logo everywhere else.

The first second counts

  • In a short video, the brand has to be heard in the first second. Douglas has a one-note cue for exactly that, plus a 3-second sound logo and a 60-second brand track, all sharing one motif.

Rights for every medium and every year

  • Ask who owns the music, in which countries and for how long. Westwing’s sound logo is registered with GEMA, with worldwide rights in all media and no time limit.

A one-off piece or a system

  • A one-off jingle covers one piece of music. A system adds research, tests with listeners and guidelines, so the next campaign does not start from zero. Decide which one you need before you compare prices.

What is the difference between a jingle and a sonic logo?

A jingle is advertising music that sings or plays the brand, often with the brand name or claim in the lyrics. A sonic logo is a short sound of one to three seconds, usually without words. Both can come from the same melody.

What does a jingle for a brand cost?

It depends on whether you buy one piece of music or a system, and on the usage rights. For orientation: EightSix publishes these ranges: about €15,000 to €30,000 for a sonic logo project and €50,000 to over €500,000 for a complete sonic identity system. The price depends on scope, complexity and usage rights.

How long does it take to make a brand jingle?

A sonic identity project at EightSix usually takes 8 to 16 weeks: two to three weeks of research, four to eight weeks of composition, two to three weeks of testing and one to two weeks for guidelines.
