The terms, in plain words

Agencies use different names for the same things. This is what they mean.

Sonic branding, audio branding, sound branding

  • Three names for the same job: deciding how a brand sounds and keeping that sound consistent. Agencies in Germany often say audio branding or sound branding. In the UK and the US, sonic branding is more common.

Sonic logo, sound logo, audio logo

  • A short sound of one to three seconds that stands for the brand, like a logo for the ear. A jingle is a sung advertising line. A sonic logo usually has no words.

Jingle

  • Advertising music that sings or plays the brand, often with the brand name or claim in the lyrics. Today a jingle usually shares its melody with the sonic logo. See the list of jingle production agencies for brands.

Radio spot and audio ad

  • An ad without pictures, on the radio, on streaming services or in podcasts. Listeners have to recognise the brand by ear, so the sonic logo matters most here. See the list of radio and audio advertising agencies.

In-store music

  • Music and sound in shops, hotels and restaurants. It can come from a licensed playlist service or be built from the brand’s own sound. See the list of in-store music agencies.

Sonic identity system

  • The whole set: sonic logo, brand music, interface and product sounds, brand voice and the guidelines for using them.

UX sound and product sound

  • The sounds an app or a device makes: start-up, confirmation, notification, error.

Music supervision and sync licensing

  • Finding and clearing existing music for a film or an ad. You need the publisher for the song and the label for the recording. EightSix explains the steps in its guide to licensing a song for an ad.

Usage rights and buyout

  • The media, the countries and the length of time in which you may use the sound. Get them in writing before work starts.

First, decide what must be yours.

A two-second logo, a full sound system, or music for one campaign: the scope decides the agency.

Then ask how they decide.

Taste is not a method. Ask how a track is judged against your brand, and who signs it off.

Next, hear it in three places.

An ad, your app and a shop floor. An identity that only works in the presentation film is not one.

Last, own it.

Buy-out, territories, duration, AI training: get the rights in writing before the first note.

What does a sonic branding agency do?

It designs how a brand sounds and keeps that sound consistent: the sonic logo, brand music, interface and product sounds, voice, and the guidelines that hold them together across advertising, apps, shops and events.

How much does sonic branding cost?

EightSix publishes these ranges: about €15,000 to €30,000 for a sonic logo project and €50,000 to €500,000 or more for a complete sonic identity system for a global brand. The price depends on scope, complexity and usage rights.

How long does a sonic branding project take?

A typical project takes 8 to 16 weeks from kickoff to delivery: two to three weeks of discovery and research, four to eight weeks of composition, two to three weeks of testing and refinement, and one to two weeks for guidelines and assets.

What is the difference between a sonic logo and sonic branding?

A sonic logo is a short, distinctive sound of one to three seconds, the audio equivalent of a visual logo. Sonic branding is the whole system: the logo plus brand music, interface sounds, voice and guidelines, built to work across all touchpoints for years.
